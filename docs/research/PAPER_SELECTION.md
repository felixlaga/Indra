# Choosing papers: search queries, candidate pools and selection

A branch no longer reads whatever a source ranks first for its query. As in ERLA's inner loop, it searches a larger candidate pool than it reads and then chooses.

## Why

A session asked a long, conversational question about where gravitational-wave lensing research is headed. The question went to OpenAlex verbatim, and OpenAlex's full-text search matched its filler words. The five papers read were "Astrophysics in 2002", an undergraduate journal issue, a student conference abstract book, an interview with John Milnor and a 1982 science-policy volume. arXiv rejected the same text with HTTP 500. Searching `gravitational wave lensing` returns the field on both sources.

## What a branch does now

1. **Search queries.** A query of up to 6 words, in one sentence, is searched as written. This covers Scout follow-ups, which are already written as short search queries. A longer question is rewritten by the model into at most 3 keyword queries plus a short topic (`SearchPlan`, one budgeted call). Without a model, or when the call fails, the topic words of the question's first sentence are used, with request words such as *gaps*, *biggest*, *direction* and *unknowns* removed. The queries and how they were made (`model`, `key_terms` or `as_written`) are kept in the job result, so a retried job reuses them.
2. **Candidate pool.** Every query runs on every selected source, each returning up to `search_limit` papers (15 by default). Results are interleaved by rank and merged by canonical key and normalized title. A merged paper keeps any abstract that a duplicate supplies. Papers already read by other branches are excluded. Each source gets its own query syntax. arXiv requires every term (`all:a AND all:b`), because a bare multi-word query matches any one of the words, and keeps quoted phrases. Semantic Scholar drops the quotes. arXiv requests are spaced 3 s apart across concurrent searches, Semantic Scholar's 1 s.
3. **Ranking.** Each candidate is scored on how many of the topic's words it contains: full weight in the title, partial weight in the abstract, with light stemming so that *lens/lensing* and *molecules/molecular* match. When `INDRA_EMBEDDING_MODEL` is set, the score is averaged with the cosine similarity between the question and the title plus abstract. Being found by several queries or sources adds a small capped bonus.
4. **Selection.** When the pool is larger than the number of papers the branch reads, the model gets the best-ranked 30 candidates (title, year, venue, citations, abstract cut to 400 characters). It chooses at most that many (`PaperSelection`, one budgeted call), preferring papers on the subject itself, a review when available, and different aspects over near-duplicates. It leaves out off-topic papers even when that means reading fewer. Unknown candidate numbers are dropped. When the model is unavailable or its answer contains no valid choice, the branch reads the best-ranked candidates. If any candidate mentions the topic, off-topic candidates are never used to fill the quota.
5. **Record.** A `paper_selection` agent decision stores the queries, the sources, the pool size, the method (`model` or `ranking`), the chosen papers with the model's reasons and up to 10 candidates not chosen. Each branch in the Scout view shows its searches and a "Why these papers" list. Each paper read keeps the model's reason as its `selection_reason`. On-topic candidates that were not read are kept as found papers for the [research graph](RESEARCH_GRAPH.md).

Planning and selection count against the branch's share of the model-call budget, like paper summaries. They run first, so the papers a branch reads are chosen before its budget is spent on them. A rate limit or exhausted quota during either call stops model use for the rest of the branch, exactly as it does for summaries.
